    How the calculator works

    Each design is a set of parallel laminar passages: the plain annular gap, or the finned regenerator’s tip clearance, slots and OD clearance. The gas the displacer sweeps divides among them in proportion to their flow conductance, and each passage’s heat transfer is judged by its NTU. The model is quasi-steady, so use it to compare designs and see trends; confirm final numbers in Sage.

    Gas flow from the displacer

    Ve = (π/4)·Dd²·s    V̂ = Ve/2    Q̂ = ω·V̂    ω = 2πf

    Helium at the regenerator’s mean temperature

    Tr = (Th − Tc) / ln(Th/Tc)    ρ = p / (R·Tr)
    μ = 1.99×10⁻⁵·(Tr/300)0.68    kg = 0.155·(Tr/300)0.71

    Pressure drop and pumping loss

    Gi = 2·Ai·dh,i² / (Ci·μ·L)    Δp̂ = Q̂ / ΣGi    φi = Gi / ΣG
    Pflow = π·f·Δp̂·V̂    ∝ f²·s²

    Regenerator leak

    NTUi = Nui·kg·Pi·L / (dh,i·ρ·φi·Q̂·cp)    ∝ 1/s
    ε′ = Σ φi·2/(NTUi + 2)    Qregen = ε′·(2/π)·ρ·Q̂·cp·(Th − Tc)

    When NTU is large, ε′ ≈ 2/NTU, which grows in proportion to s, while the flow Q̂ also grows with s. So Qregen ≈ c·s²: doubling the stroke roughly quadruples the leak.

    Steel conduction

    Qcond = ks·(Aregen − Aremoved)·(Th − Tc) / L    ks ≈ 14.9 + 0.01325·T̄ (°C)

    This does not depend on stroke at all.

    Break-even stroke

    cplain·s*² = cfinned·s*² + Qcond  ⇒  s* ≈ √( Qcond / (cplain − cfinned) )

    Below s* the finned regenerator loses more heat than it saves; above s* it wins. The calculator finds s* numerically from the full model.

    Engine performance

    Pideal = p·ω·V0·Ve·sinθ / (8·Veff) · (Th − Tc)/Th    Qideal = Pideal·Th/(Th − Tc)    (Chapter 6)
    Pelec = ηalt·(Pideal − Pflow − kother·Pideal)    Qin = Qideal + Qregen + Qcond + Qother    η = Pelec/Qin

    The finned regenerator changes the engine three ways: a smaller displacer and extra gas volume lower the ideal power (through Ve and Veff), its pumping loss takes work directly, and its better regeneration cuts the heat that has to be supplied. Mechanical losses outside the regenerator are a fixed share of ideal power for both designs.

    Working gas

    ρ = p/(R·Tr)    NTU ∝ kg/(ρ·cp)    δg = √(2·kg/(ρ·cp·ω))

    Helium: R = 2077 J/kg·K, cp = 5193 J/kg·K. Air: R = 287 J/kg·K, cp ≈ 1007 + 0.13·(T − 300) J/kg·K. At the same pressure and temperature, air conducts heat about 6 times less well and stores about 1.4 times more heat per unit volume, so its thermal diffusivity is about 8 times lower. Gaps sized for helium are far too wide for air. Chapter 6’s ideal power doesn’t depend on the gas, but its cavity heat-transfer losses scale with gap² ÷ diffusivity, so set “Other mechanical losses in air” higher than the helium value.

    Fins from the EDM slots

    Droot = DID + 2·d    tfin(D) = π·D/N − b    twall = (DOD − Droot)/2
    Aslots = N·b·d    Afins = (π/4)·(Droot² − DID²) − N·b·d    pitch = 360°/N
    kerf = wire Ø + 2·spark gap    r = kerf/2 (the radius the wire leaves)
    Contoured: b ≥ kerf, Aslot = b·d − (2 − π/2)·r²    Plunge: b = kerf, Aslot = b·(d − r) + π·r²/2

    Wire EDM cuts parallel-sided slots, so the fins left between them taper: thinnest at the bore, thickest at the slot root. A contoured slot is cut by moving the wire across the slot, which leaves two root corners of radius r. A plunged slot is cut by feeding the wire straight to depth: it is one kerf wide with a full-round bottom, and takes much less wire travel.

    Annular gap

    c = (Dtip − Dd)/2    total = (Dhousing − Dd)/2 = c + hfin + twall + cOD
    gmin = c − e    gmax = c + e    Qoff-center/Qcentered = 1 + 1.5·(e/c)²

    An off-center displacer pushes more gas through the wide side of the tip gap, bypassing the slots. Worst-case narrowest gap: gmin = (smallest tip bore − largest displacer OD)/2 − (regenerator float + runout + displacer centering).

    Pressure swing (Chapter 6, Equation 6.1)

    Δpswing ∝ Ve / (Vm − Vh·F)    F = (Th − Tc)/Th    ΔVeff = ΔV·(1 − F/2)
